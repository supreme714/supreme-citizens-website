JANUARY 6: MEMORY, RECORD & REPUBLIC

Elections. Transfer of Power. Constitutional Government. Historical Memory.

On January 6, 2021, Congress assembled at the United States Capitol to count the electoral votes submitted by the states. The proceeding followed weeks of election challenges, litigation, political disagreement and competing claims concerning the 2020 presidential election. While Congress was conducting the count, the Capitol was breached and the proceeding stopped. Hours later, Congress returned, resumed the electoral count and completed the constitutional process.

Evidence Standard

How we handle the record

Established Fact

Supported by reliable evidence.

Primary Source

Original or contemporaneous record.

Court Finding

A judicial determination.

Government Finding

An official investigative conclusion.

Allegation

A claim not established merely by assertion.

Disputed

Material disagreement remains.

Conflicting Evidence

Reliable records materially conflict.

Later Development

Later evidence or law changed the record.

“

Memory, Record & Republic

What can January 6 teach us?

The purpose of remembering January 6 is larger than remembering one day. It is an opportunity to understand how constitutional government operates when elections are disputed, institutions are tested and citizens profoundly disagree.
