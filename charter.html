THE SUPREME CITIZENS CHARTER

A Charter of Constitutional Citizenship, Popular Sovereignty, Equal Civic Dignity, Representative Stewardship, Institutional Independence, Governmental Accountability, and Responsibility to Posterity

BRANDED CIVIC EDITION

FOUNDATIONAL STATEMENT

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The Supreme Citizens Charter is a civic framework. It does not create a new legal status, confer personal sovereignty, place citizens above the law, or authorize resistance to lawful governmental authority.

It is founded upon the American constitutional principle that legitimate political authority ultimately originates with We the People, while governmental power is exercised through institutions created, divided, and limited by the Constitution.

“The people are sovereign. The Constitution is supreme. Citizens possess equal civic dignity. Governmental power is divided. Public office is temporary stewardship. Citizenship carries both rights and responsibilities.”

PREAMBLE

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

We, the citizens of the United States, inheritors and temporary custodians of a constitutional republic, affirm that government exists under the authority of the people and within the constitutional framework established for their common government.

We recognize that republican government does not create rulers who own public institutions. It creates offices through which citizens temporarily entrust governmental authority to representatives, executives, judges, administrators, and other public servants.

We affirm that every citizen possesses equal civic dignity.

No American becomes inherently more entitled to political participation or constitutional protection because of wealth, race, ethnicity, sex, religion, ancestry, occupation, social standing, political affiliation, or whether citizenship was obtained by birth or lawful naturalization, except for constitutional qualifications expressly applicable to particular offices.

We acknowledge that constitutional liberty cannot be preserved by written guarantees alone. It requires citizens who understand their rights; accept their responsibilities; participate; seek evidence; question authority; defend the rights of those with whom they disagree; demand integrity from those exercising public power; and distinguish constitutional loyalty from personal or partisan loyalty.

We recognize that presidents, members of Congress, judges, governors, mayors, legislators, administrators, military officers, law-enforcement officials, and other public servants are temporary stewards of institutions belonging to the Republic.

Their offices are entrusted. Their powers are limited. Their responsibilities are public. The Republic is greater than any officeholder.

We recognize that executive unity must never be confused with governmental supremacy. The President leads the executive branch. Congress remains a coordinate legislative branch. The judiciary remains an independent constitutional branch. The states retain constitutional authority within the federal system. And the people remain the source of political legitimacy.

We further recognize that constitutional institutions may be weakened not only through formal seizures of authority, but also through fear, dependency, intimidation, retaliation, personal loyalty, institutional passivity, or voluntary surrender of constitutional responsibility.

Accordingly, we establish this Charter to promote constitutional citizenship, institutional independence, peaceful political disagreement, governmental accountability, civic participation, protected liberty, and responsibility to future generations.

“We are Supreme Citizens not because any one of us stands above another, but because together we are We the People.”

ARTICLE I DECLARATION OF PRINCIPLES

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1Popular Sovereignty

Legitimate political authority ultimately originates with the people. Government does not confer popular sovereignty upon citizens as a political favor. Through the Constitution, the people establish institutions and entrust them with particular responsibilities.

2Equal Citizenship

Every citizen possesses equal civic dignity. There shall be no aristocracy of citizenship. Political disagreement does not diminish citizenship.

3Constitutional Supremacy

No President, Congress, court, political party, government agency, political movement, donor, corporation, or individual citizen stands above the Constitution.

4Distributed Constitutional Power

Federal governmental authority is intentionally divided. Congress exercises legislative authority. The President exercises executive authority. The judiciary exercises judicial authority. No branch is the government itself.

5Unitary but Limited Executive Authority

The President leads the executive branch. Executive unity does not erase the independent constitutional authority of Congress, the judiciary, or the states.

6Temporary Public Stewardship

Public office is a temporary public trust. An election does not create ownership. An appointment does not create personal dominion. An inauguration does not create a sovereign.

7Authority Requires Accountability

The greater the authority exercised by an officeholder or institution, the greater the corresponding obligation to accept responsibility for its use.

8Protected Liberty and Rule of Law

Citizens and public officials alike remain subject to lawful constitutional authority. Constitutional protection must apply even when the person receiving it is politically unpopular.

9Checks, Balances, and Institutional Independence

Checks and balances are safeguards against concentrated power.

10Electoral and Civic Accountability

Citizenship does not begin and end at the ballot box. Self-government requires involvement before, during, and after elections.

11Constitution Before Party

Political parties organize political competition. They do not possess constitutional sovereignty.

12Posterity

Every generation temporarily possesses stewardship of institutions it did not create alone. The Republic must be preserved for those who follow.

ARTICLE II THE MEANING OF “SUPREME CITIZENS”

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1Collective Popular Sovereignty

The term Supreme Citizens refers to the collective political sovereignty of the people operating through constitutional government.

2Equality, Not Hierarchy

No Supreme Citizen is supreme over another. The concept is founded upon equal citizenship.

3Participation, Not Exemption

Supreme Citizens participate within constitutional government. They do not claim exemption from lawful courts, taxes, licenses, public obligations, or governmental jurisdiction.

4Accountability, Not Anarchy

The Charter does not seek weak or ineffective government. It seeks effective government under constitutional accountability.

5Distinction From Sovereign Citizen Ideology

The Supreme Citizens Charter expressly rejects so-called Sovereign Citizen ideology. The Charter advocates participation rather than exemption; constitutional accountability rather than personal sovereignty; and rule of law rather than rejection of lawful institutions.

ARTICLE III RIGHTS OF CITIZENSHIP

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1Protected Civic Liberties

The Charter does not itself create legally enforceable rights. It articulates civic principles associated with rights protected by the Constitution, amendments, federal and state law, and the American constitutional system.

2Rights Citizens Should Understand and Defend

Freedom of speech; freedom of religion and conscience; freedom of the press; peaceful assembly; association; petition; due process; equal protection; lawful electoral participation; privacy consistent with constitutional guarantees; access to judicial process; access to public information subject to lawful limitations; and the freedom to criticize and question governmental authority.

3The Right to Question Power

A government office does not create entitlement to personal obedience. Citizens may question presidents, Congress, courts, policies, and public institutions through lawful political, civic, and judicial processes.

ARTICLE IV RESPONSIBILITIES OF CITIZENSHIP

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1Civic Responsibilities

Supreme Citizens should become reasonably informed; understand the Constitution and structure of government; participate in elections when eligible; remain engaged between elections; evaluate competing information critically; seek evidence before spreading consequential claims; distinguish reporting from opinion and evidence from propaganda; contact representatives; attend public meetings when appropriate; request public information; serve on juries when called; participate in community organizations; protect the rights of others; reject political violence; respect lawful processes for resolving election disputes; and preserve constitutional institutions for future generations.

2Information Discipline

Before accepting an important political claim, citizens should ask: Who made the claim? What evidence supports it? Is significant context missing? Can it be corroborated independently? Would I apply the same evidentiary standard if the claim harmed my preferred political party?

ARTICLE V REPRESENTATIVE STEWARDSHIP

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1Public Office Is a Public Trust

Every elected and appointed official exercises temporary authority on behalf of the Republic.

2The Office Is Greater Than the Officeholder

The presidency is greater than any President. Congress is greater than any legislator. The judiciary is greater than any judge. The Republic is greater than any political party.

3Constitutional Loyalty

No public official owes personal allegiance to another public official above the Constitution and lawful responsibilities of office.

4Competence

Public officials should exercise authority with competence, diligence, preparation, professionalism, and judgment.

5Truthfulness

Public officials should not knowingly deceive citizens regarding material matters of public consequence.

6Transparency

Government should explain major decisions when lawful considerations such as national security, privacy, or legitimate law-enforcement requirements do not require confidentiality.

7Responsibility to the Entire Jurisdiction

Winning office does not eliminate responsibility toward people who voted for another candidate.

8Avoidance of Self-Dealing

Public authority should not primarily serve personal enrichment, political revenge, family advantage, partisan entrenchment, or personal glorification.

9Acceptance of Lawful Oversight

Oversight is part of constitutional government. It is not inherently an attack upon executive legitimacy.

10Peaceful Departure From Office

Temporary stewardship requires accepting lawful termination of public authority.

ARTICLE VI EXECUTIVE POWER AND CONSTITUTIONAL ACCOUNTABILITY

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1Executive Unity

The Constitution establishes one President. Executive unity may provide clear responsibility, administrative coordination, and accountability.

2Executive Unity Is Not Presidential Sovereignty

The President directs the executive branch within constitutional limits. That authority does not convert the presidency into sovereignty over Congress, courts, states, elections, appropriations, or the people.

3Public Institutions Are Not Personal Property

Executive agencies, public records, appropriated funds, federal employees, intelligence institutions, federal law enforcement, and the armed forces belong to the United States, not personally to the President.

4Faithful Execution

Presidential authority exists to execute governmental responsibilities within constitutional and statutory boundaries.

5Executive Orders

Executive orders may implement lawful executive authority. They are not constitutionally interchangeable with legislation where legislative authority belongs to Congress.

6Appropriations

Congress retains important constitutional authority over federal spending. Executive and judicial exercises of power remain subject to valid constitutional and statutory limitations.

7Emergency Authority

Emergency authority should remain legally grounded, proportionate, reviewable, temporary, and subject to applicable oversight.

8Law Enforcement

Federal law enforcement serves the United States under law. It should not become a personal instrument for rewarding allies or retaliating against opponents.

9Military Authority

The President is Commander in Chief, while Congress possesses substantial constitutional powers concerning war, appropriations, the armed forces, and related regulation.

10Professional Public Administration

Executive accountability and professional public service can coexist. Political leadership may establish lawful policy while career officials provide expertise, continuity, and administration.

ARTICLE VII LEGISLATIVE STEWARDSHIP AND INSTITUTIONAL INDEPENDENCE

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1Congress Is a Coordinate Branch

Members of Congress are not employees of the President. Congress possesses independent legislative, appropriations, oversight, impeachment, and other constitutional responsibilities.

2Oversight Must Survive Partisanship

Congressional oversight should not cease because the President belongs to the congressional majority's political party.

3Institutional Stewardship

Legislators should preserve the authority of Congress regardless of partisan alignment with the President.

4Impeachment Remains a Constitutional Check

The Constitution assigns the House the sole power of impeachment and the Senate the authority to try impeachments, providing an institutional mechanism for accountability of civil officers.

5Responsible Delegation

Congress should carefully consider the powers it delegates to the executive branch and periodically evaluate whether delegated authority remains appropriately bounded.

ARTICLE VIII THE PRINCIPLE OF INSTITUTIONAL INDEPENDENCE

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

No President, political party, elected official, movement, donor, corporation, organization, or individual should command personal loyalty above constitutional duty.

Public officials are entrusted with independent responsibilities and should exercise their judgment without improper intimidation, coercion, retaliation, or demands for personal allegiance.

“The preservation of the Republic requires not merely constitutional institutions, but public officials willing to exercise the independent authority entrusted to them.”

ARTICLE IX CIVIC WARNING MODEL: POLITICAL COERCIVE CONTROL

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The Charter uses coercive control only as a civic analytical model. It does not assert that a political party literally occupies the role of an abused spouse, nor should the serious realities of domestic abuse be trivialized through casual political analogy.

The political comparison is narrower. It asks whether mechanisms such as dependency, intimidation, loyalty demands, retaliation, isolation, boundary testing, and suppression of independent judgment are reducing the ability of constitutional actors to perform their responsibilities independently.

These signs should be applied to any President, party, movement, legislative majority, governor, or political organization, regardless of ideology.

TWELVE CIVIC WARNING SIGNS

1Personal Loyalty Replaces Constitutional Loyalty

Officials begin evaluating decisions according to personal loyalty to a leader rather than constitutional responsibilities, legal obligations, constituents, evidence, and independent judgment.

2Disagreement Becomes Betrayal

Healthy political parties contain disagreements. The warning sign appears when disagreement itself becomes evidence of disloyalty and retaliation becomes expected.

3Fear Influences Constitutional Judgment

Officials may reasonably consider electoral consequences. The warning condition exists when anticipated retaliation becomes sufficiently powerful that representatives suppress constitutional or policy judgments they would otherwise express.

4Rewards and Punishments Become Instruments of Personal Compliance

Political endorsements and campaign support are legitimate parts of democratic politics. The concern arises when governmental independence becomes materially dependent upon personal loyalty to one leader.

5Independent Voices Become Isolated

The civic concern arises when ideological diversity becomes secondary to whether officials are personally loyal to one leader rather than accountable to constitutional principles, constituents, and evidence.

6Officials Begin “Walking on Eggshells”

The political analogue occurs when officials consistently soften, conceal, or avoid disagreement because of anticipated political retaliation. Patterns matter more than isolated examples.

7Political Power Attempts to Define Reality

Citizens should distinguish ordinary political falsehoods from a broader institutional pressure to affirm propositions contradicted by reliable public records, court findings, election certifications, scientific evidence, intelligence assessments, or other verifiable sources.

8Institutions Become Dependent Upon One Leader

Political influence is normal. Dependency is more consequential. Citizens should examine whether endorsements, fundraising, campaign appearances, media ecosystems, activist networks, or protection from primary opposition become so concentrated that independent judgment carries prohibitive political cost.

9Constitutional Boundaries Are Repeatedly Tested

Citizens should ask not only whether an action is lawful today, but what constitutional precedent acquiescence establishes for future administrations.

10The Legislature Surrenders Its Own Authority

Congress can lose practical influence without formally losing constitutional power when representatives become unwilling to exercise powers entrusted to them.

11Independence Appears Only After Political Consequences Disappear

If legislators become significantly more outspoken only after retirement or electoral defeat, citizens may reasonably ask whether reelection incentives affected earlier conduct.

12The Leader Becomes More Important Than the Institution

The most serious warning condition occurs when political identity progresses from party, to movement, to personal political movement, to institutional loyalty centered on one person.

ARTICLE X THE POLITICAL COERCION TEST

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Whenever a President or other powerful political leader seeks significant institutional action, Supreme Citizens should ask:

Constitutional Test — Does the office possess the authority being claimed?

Institutional Independence Test — Is the other branch exercising its own constitutional judgment?

Retaliation Test — Are officials facing personal or political retaliation merely for exercising legitimate independent judgment?

Dependency Test — Has access to political resources become so concentrated that institutional independence carries unusual political cost?

Precedent Test — What constitutional or institutional precedent will this establish?

Reversibility Test — Would supporters accept the same authority if exercised by a leader from the opposing party?

Citizen Test — Does the action strengthen government accountable to citizens or concentrate practical authority around one individual?

No single answer automatically proves authoritarianism, coercion, illegality, or constitutional violation. The test is intended to encourage systematic civic scrutiny.

ARTICLE XI THE SUPREME CITIZENS REVERSIBILITY TEST

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

“Would I support this exact power if it were exercised by a President, Congress, governor, administration, or political party I strongly opposed?”

This test separates the person from the office; the party from the power; and the immediate political advantage from the permanent constitutional precedent.

“The question is not merely: Who possesses this power today? The more durable question is: Should this office possess this power tomorrow?”

ARTICLE XII THE POLITICAL COUNTERARGUMENT

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

For intellectual integrity, the Charter must distinguish between political influence and political coercion.

A strong party leader may legitimately win support among party voters, endorse candidates, raise campaign funds, campaign against incumbents, promote a legislative agenda, encourage party unity, and seek candidates who share that agenda.

Supporters may reasonably argue that elected officials follow a President's agenda because voters support that agenda, not because politicians are coerced. Primary challenges are normal democratic competition. Endorsements are protected political activity. Party discipline exists across American political parties.

Accordingly, the Charter does not declare that supporters of any particular President are being controlled. Instead, it establishes a party-neutral proposition:

“Citizens should examine whether any President's political influence becomes sufficiently powerful to compromise the independent constitutional judgment of Congress or other institutions.”

That same standard must apply to Democratic Presidents, Republican Presidents, independent candidates, congressional majorities, governors, and political organizations of every ideology.

ARTICLE XIII UNITARY EXECUTIVE THEORY AND POLITICAL CONCENTRATION

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A conventional version of unitary executive theory concerns presidential supervision of the executive branch. It does not by itself establish that the President controls Congress; may disregard statutes; may ignore judicial orders; possesses Congress's appropriations authority; or possesses sovereignty over the Republic.

“The Charter therefore distinguishes EXECUTIVE UNITY from EXECUTIVE SUPREMACY. These are not synonymous.”

The constitutional concern becomes more substantial when several political conditions combine:

Strong presidential control over the executive branch

A politically compliant congressional majority

Weak legislative oversight

High intraparty costs for dissent

Broad delegations of congressional authority

Together, these conditions can produce a substantial concentration of practical presidential power even though the constitutional text has not formally changed. This does not itself prove illegality. It identifies why institutional independence is necessary.

ARTICLE XIV ELECTORAL ACCOUNTABILITY

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1The Ballot Delegates Temporary Authority

Voting selects representatives. It does not surrender the political sovereignty of the people.

2No Permanent Mandate

Election victory does not confer permanent ownership of public institutions.

3Informed Voting

Citizens should consider constitutional fidelity, competence, honesty, judgment, ethics, policy, fiscal responsibility, respect for rights, institutional stewardship, and respect for lawful transfers of authority.

4Accessible and Secure Elections

Election systems should pursue both meaningful access and legitimate integrity.

5Accountability Between Elections

Citizens retain the ability to petition, advocate, investigate, organize, protest peacefully, pursue litigation, support legislation, oppose legislation, and prepare for subsequent elections.

ARTICLE XVIII THE STANDARD OF A SUPREME CITIZEN

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Informed without becoming unquestioning.

Patriotic without surrendering independent judgment.

Skeptical of concentrated power while respecting lawful institutions.

Protective of personal liberty and equally protective of the liberty of others.

Committed to facts even when facts are politically inconvenient.

Prepared to criticize government and equally prepared to participate in improving it.

Committed to voting while understanding that citizenship extends beyond elections.

Committed to Constitution before party.

Committed to the Republic before political personality.

A Supreme Citizen repeatedly asks:

What does the evidence show?

What does the Constitution authorize?

What does the law require?

Which institution has responsibility?

Who is accountable?

Are officials acting independently?

Would I support this same power in the hands of someone I oppose?

What precedent will this establish?

ARTICLE XIX COVENANT WITH POSTERITY

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

We acknowledge that the Republic did not begin with us and must not end with us.

We therefore undertake to leave future Americans free elections; peaceful political transitions; independent constitutional institutions; representative legislatures; accountable executive government; independent courts; freedom of speech; equal citizenship; due process; lawful dissent; professional public administration; reliable public information; civil peace; and the continuing opportunity to govern themselves.

Democracy rarely depends upon one moment alone. It may weaken gradually when powers once questioned become normalized; when representatives become reluctant to dissent; when political loyalty becomes personal loyalty; when institutions surrender responsibilities they were created to exercise; and when citizens begin treating protection of a leader as equivalent to protection of the country.

“No political leader is the Republic.”

“No political party is the Republic.”

“No generation owns the Republic.”

“The Republic belongs to We the People and is held in trust for those who follow.”

THE SUPREME CITIZENS CREED

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

We are Supreme Citizens.

Not because we stand above one another.

Not because we stand above the law.

Not because we claim personal sovereignty over government.

We are Supreme Citizens because together we are We the People.

We possess equal citizenship.

We possess constitutional rights.

We accept civic responsibilities.

We recognize that governmental authority comes from the people and operates through the Constitution.

We recognize that Presidents are temporary. Members of Congress are temporary. Political majorities are temporary. Administrations are temporary. Political parties are temporary. But the Republic must endure.

We will remain informed. We will vote. We will participate. We will question authority. We will examine evidence. We will resist deliberate misinformation.

We will protect constitutional rights and defend those rights even when they protect people with whom we disagree.

We will demand accountability from those entrusted with public authority.

We will reject political violence.

We will respect lawful elections and lawful processes for contesting them.

We will defend independent constitutional institutions.

We will reject demands for personal political allegiance above constitutional duty.

We will remain vigilant when fear, intimidation, dependency, retaliation, or personal loyalty threatens independent governmental judgment.

We will place Constitution before party.

We will judge power by principle rather than personality.

We will ask whether we would accept the same governmental authority in the hands of those we politically oppose.

We recognize that a President may lead a strong and unified executive branch. But the President does not own the government.

Congress must remain independent. The judiciary must remain independent. The rule of law must remain intact. The military serves the Constitution. Public law enforcement serves the law. Public institutions belong to the Republic. And the people must remain engaged.

We do not seek powerless government. We seek accountable government.

We do not seek citizens above the law. We seek equal citizens protected by and responsible under the law.

We do not seek political domination. We seek constitutional self-government.

We recognize public office as stewardship. We recognize governmental authority as temporary. We recognize citizenship as enduring.

We hold this Republic in trust for generations we will never meet.

We pledge to leave them a nation in which they too may speak freely, vote freely, worship freely, disagree peacefully, question authority, hold leaders accountable, and participate fully in constitutional government.

We are the People.

We are all Supreme Citizens.

Our representatives are temporary.

Our citizenship endures.

Our institutions must remain independent.

The Constitution is our framework.

The Republic is ours to protect.
