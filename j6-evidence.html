Part VI · Memory, Record & Republic

EVIDENCE LEDGER
& SOURCE VAULT

A historical record is only as useful as its sources. This section identifies where evidence came from, what it can establish, what it cannot establish, and how later evidence may change the record.

Evidence-first research

The record and the evidence behind it

Evidence Ledger

Stable evidence IDs connect historical claims to documents, records and official findings.

Source Vault

Sources are organized by institution, proceeding and event so readers can examine the underlying record.

Claim Tracker

Claims are kept separate from evidence and classified according to what the record actually supports.

Governing principle: preserve the source, preserve its context, identify its limitations, and let readers examine the record themselves.
Phase 2 · Initial Evidence Ledger

Verified records J6-E-0001 through J6-E-0006

Open any record to review its provenance, limited evidentiary value, limitations and official source. These entries distinguish primary records from later government findings.

J6-E-0001Official 2020 Electoral College Results
PRIMARY SOURCEOFFICIAL GOVERNMENT RECORD

Date: December 2020–January 2021

Originator: National Archives and Records Administration

Establishes: The official Electoral College result recorded for the 2020 presidential election: Joseph R. Biden Jr. 306 electoral votes and Donald J. Trump 232.

Does not establish: This record alone does not resolve every allegation or legal argument raised about election administration; it documents the official electoral certificates and result.

Provenance: National Archives Electoral College records.

View Official Source
J6-E-0002Congressional Record — Joint Session of January 6–7, 2021
PRIMARY SOURCECONGRESSIONAL RECORD

Date: January 6–7, 2021

Originator: United States Congress / Government Publishing Office

Establishes: The official congressional record of proceedings surrounding the counting of electoral votes, including objections, debate, votes, recess and resumption.

Does not establish: Statements by individual members are arguments entered into the record; their inclusion does not itself make each assertion an adjudicated fact.

View Official Source
J6-E-0003Arizona Electoral Vote Objection and Senate Vote
PRIMARY SOURCECONGRESSIONAL RECORD

Date: January 6, 2021

Originator: United States Senate / Congress

Establishes: An objection to Arizona's electoral votes was considered and the Senate rejected the objection 93–6.

Limitation: The vote establishes the Senate's disposition of the objection; it is not a judicial ruling on every underlying factual allegation.

View Official Source
J6-E-0004Arizona Electoral Vote Objection and House Vote
PRIMARY SOURCECONGRESSIONAL RECORD

Date: January 6, 2021

Originator: United States House of Representatives / Congress

Establishes: The House rejected the objection to Arizona's electoral votes 303–121.

Limitation: The vote records the House's constitutional action on the objection and should not be described as a court adjudication.

View Official Source
J6-E-0005GAO Review of Federal Threat Information Before January 6
GOVERNMENT FINDINGINTELLIGENCE & SECURITY

Date: 2023

Originator: U.S. Government Accountability Office

Establishes: GAO reported that all 10 federal agencies it reviewed identified potential threats before January 6 and found shortcomings in how some threat information was processed or shared.

Does not establish: This later oversight finding should not be represented as proof that every agency possessed the same information, assessed it identically, or predicted the precise events that occurred.

View Official Source
J6-E-0006GAO Open-Source Threat Intelligence Review
GOVERNMENT FINDINGLATER DEVELOPMENT

Date: 2023

Originator: U.S. Government Accountability Office

Establishes: GAO documented examples of open-source information available before January 6 that indicated potential violence and reviewed how agencies handled such information.

Limitation: Availability of an online statement does not by itself establish that a particular official saw it, credited it, or could have acted on it without additional context.

View Official Source

Correction standard: Each evidence ID remains stable. Material corrections, superseding rulings, later testimony or newly authenticated records should be appended rather than silently replacing the historical record.
