Part VI · Memory, Record & Republic

EVIDENCE LEDGER
& SOURCE VAULT

A historical record is only as useful as its sources. This section establishes a transparent method for identifying where evidence came from, what it can establish, what it cannot establish, whether it has been challenged, and how later evidence changes the record.

Two connected research tools

The record and the evidence behind it

Evidence Ledger

A structured catalog of individual documents, recordings, photographs, testimony, court filings, communications, reports and other evidence. Each item receives a stable record ID and provenance information.

Source Vault

Organized collections of primary and secondary sources grouped by subject, institution, proceeding and event so readers can move from a historical claim to the underlying record.

Claim Tracker

A companion index connecting specific historical claims to the evidence supporting, disputing, contradicting or leaving them unresolved.

The governing principle: preserve the source, preserve its context, identify its limitations, and let readers examine the record themselves.
Evidence record standard

Every item should answer the same questions

1 — What is it?

Identify the document, recording, image, testimony, filing, communication or other source.

2 — Who created it?

Identify the person, court, agency, congressional body, organization or other originator.

3 — When?

Record the event date, creation date, filing date or publication date as appropriate.

4 — Provenance?

Document where the item was obtained and whether the project is linking to an original source, authenticated copy or later reproduction.

5 — What does it establish?

State the limited factual proposition directly supported by the item without extending beyond the evidence.

6 — What does it not establish?

Identify conclusions that cannot responsibly be drawn from the item alone.

7 — Is it disputed?

Identify meaningful authenticity, interpretation, completeness or contextual disputes.

8 — Was it adjudicated?

Identify whether a court actually considered the evidence and, if so, what the court decided about an issue before it.

9 — Later information?

Connect corrections, subsequent testimony, later rulings, additional footage or other evidence that changes how the item should be understood.

Stable identification

A record that can be cited and corrected

Evidence ID: J6-E-0001

Title: Descriptive source title

Event date: Date associated with the event

Source type: Court record / official government record / video / audio / communication / testimony / other

Original source: Direct source location when available

Archive date: Date preserved or entered into the ledger

People / organizations: Relevant persons and institutions

Related claims / cases: Cross-references to the Claim Tracker and legal proceedings

Evidence status: Classification based on provenance and corroboration

Correction history: Transparent record of material changes

A stable identifier allows a source to remain traceable even if its title, description, classification or interpretation is later corrected.

Source hierarchy

Start as close to the event as possible

PrioritySource classUse
1Original primary sourceOriginal document, recording, photograph, filing, certificate, communication or contemporaneous data.
2Contemporaneous official recordRecords created by responsible government institutions near the time of the event.
3Court record / sworn testimonyEvidence and statements made within formal legal or sworn proceedings, with the procedural posture identified.
4Government investigative recordLater reports and findings that synthesize evidence; conclusions are identified as those of the issuing institution.
5Contemporaneous journalismUseful for chronology and context, but checked against primary material whenever possible.
6Later scholarshipHistorical synthesis and interpretation informed by an expanded record.
7Political / advocacy interpretationUseful for documenting arguments and viewpoints, but not substituted for underlying evidence.

Claim Tracker

Separate the claim from the evidence

Public debate often begins with a conclusion and works backward. The Claim Tracker reverses that process. Each significant claim receives its own record and is connected to the evidence for and against it.

ESTABLISHED

Supported by sufficiently reliable evidence and not meaningfully contradicted by the current record.

SUPPORTED

Meaningful evidence supports the claim, but limitations or unresolved issues remain.

DISPUTED

Material disagreement exists over the evidence, context or interpretation.

CONTRADICTED

Reliable evidence materially conflicts with the claim.

UNRESOLVED

The available record does not responsibly permit a firm conclusion.

UPDATED

Later evidence materially changes an earlier classification or description; the prior history remains visible.

Compare the Record

Put competing evidence side by side

When accounts conflict, the project should not hide one version or collapse disagreement into a false certainty. A comparison view should place the relevant sources beside one another and identify their dates, provenance, evidentiary status and points of agreement or conflict.

Meaningful disagreement belongs in the record. Presenting conflicting evidence is not the same as declaring all claims equally supported.
Corrections & update history

The record must be capable of changing

New court rulings, newly released documents, additional video, corrected transcripts, later investigative findings and authenticated records may change what is known. Material corrections should be dated and preserved so readers can see both the current description and how it changed.

Correction date

When was the record changed?

What changed?

Identify the prior wording, classification or factual statement that was revised.

Why?

Link the new evidence, ruling or authoritative correction that required the update.

Research integrity

You should never have to take the project's word for it

Whenever practicable, a factual claim should lead to the original document, official record, court filing, recording or other underlying evidence. The project's role is to organize and explain the record—not to substitute itself for the record.
