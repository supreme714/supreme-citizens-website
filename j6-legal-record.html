Phase 5 · Investigations, Courts & Legal Record

LEGAL RECORD

Procedural history preserved without turning allegations, findings, judgments, dismissals and clemency into the same thing.

How to read this record

Each J6-L entry records a distinct procedural stage. Later rulings or clemency may change current legal consequences, but they do not silently erase the fact that an earlier charge, plea, verdict, judgment or dismissal occurred.

J6-L-0001
Congressional investigation

House Select Committee investigation and final report

CONGRESSIONAL FINDINGGOVERNMENT RECORD

The House Select Committee gathered testimony, documents and other materials, issued findings and recommendations, and published a final report and supporting archive.

Evidence: J6-E-0007

Limitation: Committee findings and referrals are legislative findings; they are not criminal convictions or judicial findings of guilt.

J6-L-0002
Federal prosecutions

Charges, pleas, trials and case dispositions

OFFICIAL GOVERNMENT RECORDCASE-SPECIFIC STATUS

Federal January 6 prosecutions produced different procedural outcomes, including charges, guilty pleas, trial convictions, acquittals, dismissals, sentences and appeals.

Evidence: J6-E-0009

Limitation: A criminal charge is an allegation. Each defendant must be described by the actual disposition of that person's case.

J6-L-0003
Conviction record

Convictions and guilty pleas remain distinct from charging records

ADJUDICATED OUTCOMECASE-SPECIFIC

Where a defendant pleaded guilty or was convicted at trial, that adjudicated result should be preserved separately from the original charging document and from any later appeal or clemency.

Evidence: J6-E-0009

Limitation: A conviction in one case does not establish guilt for another defendant, another charge, or a broader political claim.

J6-L-0004
Supreme Court statutory interpretation

Fischer v. United States narrowed 18 U.S.C. §1512(c)(2)

COURT FINDINGLATER DEVELOPMENT

The Supreme Court narrowed the circumstances in which the obstruction provision in 18 U.S.C. §1512(c)(2) may be applied, affecting a subset of January 6 cases.

Evidence: J6-E-0010

Limitation: Fischer did not erase every January 6 prosecution or invalidate unrelated charges, convictions or conduct.

J6-L-0005
Supreme Court presidential-immunity framework

Trump v. United States established an immunity framework

COURT FINDINGLATER DEVELOPMENT

The Supreme Court established a framework distinguishing exclusive constitutional authority, other official acts and unofficial acts when evaluating criminal prosecution of a former president.

Evidence: Supreme Court opinion

Limitation: The decision did not itself determine guilt or innocence on the federal election-related allegations.

J6-L-0006
Federal election case disposition

Federal election-related prosecution ended without a trial verdict

DISMISSEDNO TRIAL VERDICT

The federal election-related case against Donald Trump was dismissed before trial after the 2024 election under the Justice Department's position concerning prosecution of a sitting president.

Evidence: Federal court / DOJ disposition record

Limitation: A dismissal before trial under a presidential-prosecution policy is not a trial conviction and is not an acquittal on the factual merits.

J6-L-0007
Executive clemency

January 2025 pardons and commutations changed legal consequences

PARDONCOMMUTATIONEXECUTIVE CLEMENCY

Presidential clemency covered federal January 6 offenses through pardons, commutations and directions concerning pending covered cases.

Evidence: J6-E-0009

Limitation: Clemency is a later executive action. It should not be presented as an appellate reversal of the historical charging, plea, verdict or sentencing record.

J6-L-0008
Procedural-history rule

Preserve the complete legal sequence

RECORD STANDARD

For significant proceedings, the record should preserve allegation or charge, adjudication, appeal, later court ruling, dismissal, clemency and current status as separate events.

Evidence: J6-E-0007 · J6-E-0009 · J6-E-0010

Limitation: Later developments should update the current status without silently overwriting what occurred earlier in the procedural history.
