Public Office Qualification & Accountability Standard

Being legally eligible to run for office answers one question. Being prepared to exercise the public's authority answers another.

Purpose

Evaluate the Office, Not the Candidate's Identity

This nonpartisan civic framework gives citizens, journalists, civic organizations, debate moderators, and candidates a consistent way to examine readiness for public office. It does not create additional legal qualifications for office and does not replace constitutional, statutory, state, or local election requirements.

Two separate questions must remain separate: May this person legally hold the office? is determined by applicable law. Should citizens entrust this person with the office? is a judgment voters make after examining knowledge, competence, ethics, judgment, accountability, and commitment to constitutional government.

Three levels

From Eligibility to Public Trust

Level I — Legal Eligibility

Does the candidate satisfy the constitutional and statutory qualifications that actually govern the office, such as age, citizenship, residency, and other lawful requirements?

Level II — Competency & Fitness

Does the candidate demonstrate sufficient understanding, judgment, preparation, and ability to perform the responsibilities and respect the limitations of the office?

Level III — Public Trust

Does the candidate demonstrate ethical judgment, accountability, transparency, respect for equal citizenship, and commitment to lawful constitutional government?

Core standards

10 Areas Every Candidate Can Be Asked About

1. Constitutional Understanding

Understanding the responsibilities, powers, and limitations of the office.

2. Rule of Law

Recognition that public officials exercise authority under law and remain accountable to it.

3. Separation of Powers

Understanding the authority of other branches and levels of government.

4. Competence

Knowledge, experience, judgment, preparation, or demonstrated ability relevant to performing the work.

5. Ethical Conduct

Understanding conflicts of interest, disclosure, recusal, and ethical responsibilities.

6. Fiscal Responsibility

Understanding the responsibility associated with authorizing, spending, overseeing, or managing public money.

7. Equal Representation

Commitment to serving constituents regardless of whether they supported the candidate.

8. Constitutional & Civil Rights

Recognition of the obligation to protect lawful rights, including those of people with whom the officeholder disagrees.

9. Truthfulness & Transparency

Commitment to accurate public communication, appropriate disclosure, and correction of significant errors.

10. Accountability

Willingness to explain decisions, acknowledge serious mistakes, and accept lawful oversight and consequences.

Standardized candidate interview

20 Questions Every Candidate Should Be Prepared to Answer

These questions are designed to be applied consistently to candidates seeking the same office. Follow-up questions may examine an answer, record, or contradiction, but the core standard should not change according to party, ideology, identity, popularity, or personal preference.

SAME OFFICE. SAME CORE QUESTIONS. SAME STANDARD.
  1. Do you meet all constitutional and statutory qualifications for the office you are seeking?
  2. What are the principal constitutional or legal responsibilities of this office?
  3. What are the most important limits on the power of this office?
  4. What responsibility would you have to uphold the Constitution and laws when you personally disagree with a policy or legal outcome?
  5. How should this office interact with other branches and levels of government?
  6. Should elected officials be subject to the same laws and lawful processes they expect citizens to obey?
  7. How would you handle a government decision that could financially or personally benefit you, your family, your business interests, or a major supporter?
  8. What ethical standards should apply to someone holding this office?
  9. What financial interests or relationships would you disclose, divest, or recuse yourself from to prevent conflicts of interest?
  10. What experience, education, training, judgment, or demonstrated abilities prepare you to perform the actual duties of this office?
  11. What process would you use to make an important decision when information is incomplete or conflicting?
  12. How would you evaluate advice from career professionals, subject-matter experts, legal counsel, constituents, and political advisers when they disagree?
  13. What responsibility does this office have for protecting taxpayer funds and public resources?
  14. How would you represent citizens who did not vote for you or who strongly disagree with you?
  15. What responsibility does this office have to protect the constitutional and civil rights of people with whom you disagree?
  16. If information you publicly presented were later shown to be materially incorrect, what responsibility would you have to correct the record?
  17. What circumstances would cause you to publicly acknowledge that you made a serious mistake?
  18. What standards should govern your response to certified election results and the lawful recount, contest, and judicial processes available to challenge them?
  19. What responsibility does an elected official have to support the lawful and peaceful transfer of governmental authority?
  20. Why should citizens entrust you with the governmental authority associated with this office?

Office-specific examination

The Questions Should Follow the Powers of the Office

The universal questions provide a common foundation. Additional questions should address the actual authority and responsibilities of the particular office.

President & Vice President

Executive authority, constitutional limits, commander-in-chief responsibilities, appointments, vetoes, emergency powers, foreign affairs, execution of federal law, and transfer of power.

U.S. House

Legislation, appropriations, oversight, constituent representation, impeachment responsibilities, and constitutional congressional authority.

U.S. Senate

Legislation, confirmations, treaties, oversight, impeachment trials, and representation of the state.

Governor

State constitutional authority, administration, budgeting, appointments, veto authority, emergencies, and relations with the legislature and local governments.

State Legislature

State lawmaking, appropriations, oversight, constituent representation, ethics, and state constitutional responsibilities.

Local & Other Elected Offices

Questions should be tailored to the powers actually granted to mayors, councils, boards, sheriffs, elected judges, school boards, and other offices under applicable law.

Journalism & civic questioning

Job-Relevant Questions, Applied Fairly

Candidate scrutiny should focus on matters materially related to exercising public authority: legal eligibility, powers and limitations, constitutional knowledge, competence, judgment, ethics, conflicts, public finances, rights, truthfulness, and accountability.

The standard does not prevent investigation of a candidate's background. Prior governmental performance, professional history, financial interests, conflicts of interest, public statements, misconduct, legal matters, and other relevant records may bear directly on fitness and public trust. The principle is relevance to public responsibility and consistent scrutiny.

What this framework rejects

Characteristics unrelated to performing the office should not be substituted for evidence of competence or fitness. Candidates for the same office should face a consistent core standard rather than a qualification test designed around a particular individual.

Citizen evaluation

Before You Vote, Ask

Is this person legally eligible?

Do they understand the office and the limits of its power?

Have they demonstrated the competence and judgment to perform its duties?

Will they protect the constitutional rights of all citizens?

Will they accept scrutiny, lawful oversight, and accountability?

Can they be trusted with the authority citizens are delegating to them?

THE CONSTITUTION AND LAW DETERMINE WHO MAY HOLD AN OFFICE.
THE CITIZENS DETERMINE WHOM THEY WILL ENTRUST WITH IT.

Your vote is not simply a choice between candidates. It is a decision about whom you will entrust with a portion of the public authority exercised in your name.

INFORM • ENGAGE • PARTICIPATE • PRESERVE

Evaluate the office. Question consistently. Decide for yourself.

Supreme Citizens does not endorse candidates, parties, or political ideologies. This framework is designed to help citizens examine qualifications and public trust using the same civic standard.
