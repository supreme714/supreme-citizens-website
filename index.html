The revised inclusive identity

Citizenship as stewardship

The Supreme Citizens identity frames citizenship as an enduring constitutional relationship—not a partisan label, social rank, or claim of personal sovereignty. A Supreme Citizen participates within lawful constitutional government, protects the rights of others, questions power, seeks truth, rejects political violence, and helps preserve the Republic for future generations.

The term Supreme Citizens refers to the collective popular sovereignty of the people. The singular a Supreme Citizen expresses the responsibilities each person carries within that shared civic body.

Equality, not hierarchy

The constitutional foundation

The 12 Principles

Twelve principles organize the Charter around popular sovereignty, equal citizenship, constitutional limits, accountable institutions, civic participation, and responsibility to posterity.

01

Popular Sovereignty

Legitimate political authority ultimately originates with the people.

02

Equal Citizenship

Every citizen possesses equal civic dignity. Political disagreement does not diminish citizenship.

03

Constitutional Supremacy

No public official, institution, party, movement, corporation, or citizen stands above the Constitution.

04

Distributed Constitutional Power

Legislative, executive, and judicial powers are divided so that no branch becomes the government itself.

05

Unitary but Limited Executive Authority

The President leads the executive branch, while Congress, the judiciary, the states, elections, and constitutional rights remain independent limits.

06

Temporary Public Stewardship

Public office is a temporary public trust—not personal ownership, dominion, or sovereignty.

07

Authority Requires Accountability

The greater the authority entrusted to an officeholder or institution, the greater the obligation to accept responsibility for its use.

08

Protected Liberty and Rule of Law

Citizens and officials alike remain subject to lawful constitutional authority, and rights apply even when their exercise is unpopular.

09

Checks, Balances, and Institutional Independence

Independent institutions and constitutional checks are safeguards against concentrated power.

10

Electoral and Civic Accountability

Citizenship does not begin and end at the ballot box; self-government requires engagement before, during, and after elections.

11

Constitution Before Party

Political parties organize competition, but they do not possess constitutional sovereignty.

12

Posterity

Each generation holds the Republic in trust and must preserve constitutional institutions for those who follow.

Representative Stewardship

Public office is a public trust.

The office is greater than the officeholder. Legitimate public leadership requires constitutional loyalty, competence, truthfulness, transparency, avoidance of self-dealing, lawful oversight, and peaceful departure from office.

Unitary but Limited Executive

Executive unity is not executive supremacy.

The President leads the executive branch, but does not acquire sovereignty over Congress, the courts, the states, elections, appropriations, or the people. Strong executive leadership and strong constitutional limits can coexist.

A standard for constitutional citizenship

Constitution before party. Principle before personality. Republic before political advantage.

Judge power by standards you would accept even when exercised by people you politically oppose.
