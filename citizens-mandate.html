What is a political mandate?

Winning an election gives a candidate the legal authority to serve in an office, subject to constitutional and legal requirements. It does not demonstrate that every voter endorsed every policy.

A claim of a mandate for a particular policy needs further evidence: the policy actually presented during the campaign, how central it was to voters, credible survey results, and the limits of the office's powers. Voters may support a candidate for different, sometimes conflicting reasons.

Electoral victory

Official results establish the outcome of a contest. A margin of victory is not, by itself, proof of agreement with every platform item.

Policy support

A policy-specific mandate is a claim to be tested with campaign statements and reliable evidence about voter priorities.

Constitutional limits

No electoral result creates authority beyond the Constitution or cancels the responsibilities of other branches.

The Citizens' Mandate Standard

Seven questions every claim should answer

This is an educational evaluation framework, not a legal determination or a government political rating.

1. Was the election validly decided?

Consult official results and certification information.

2. How broad was the victory?

Check vote share, margin, participation, and the relevant electorate.

3. What did the candidate promise?

Find the original campaign statement, platform or debate record.

4. What motivated voters?

Consider credible research. Do not assume every voter had the same reason.

5. Was the policy clear?

Determine whether the policy was specific and central enough to evaluate.

6. What authority does the office possess?

Distinguish lawful powers from promises requiring legislative or other action.

7. What happened after the election?

Examine official actions, documented outcomes, obstacles and public explanations.

One standard for everyone. These questions apply regardless of candidate, political party, ideology, margin of victory or personal political preference.

The Citizens' Mandate declaration

An election determines who exercises public authority. It does not transfer ownership of government from the people to those elected.

Our continuing civic responsibility is to learn, participate, examine the record, respect constitutional processes and the equal rights of others, and hold public institutions accountable through lawful means.

We the People Are Not an Audience.

The Citizens Branch is a civic participation concept, not a fourth constitutionally established federal branch. The Supreme Citizens Charter is a nonpartisan educational initiative, not a governmental authority or legal advisory service.
