Part VIII · Memory, Record & Republic

YOU DECIDE

A republic depends on citizens who can examine evidence, distinguish facts from allegations and interpretations, understand what institutions actually decided, and reach informed conclusions without surrendering judgment to a political party, personality, headline or website.

The civic purpose

This is not a political quiz

The purpose of this section is not to tell readers what they must believe about January 6. It is to make the strongest available record easier to examine. Each major question should be approached using the same structure.

Examine the claim

State the question precisely enough that evidence can actually support, contradict or leave it unresolved.

Examine the record

Review supporting evidence, contrary evidence, primary sources, court rulings, government findings and important limitations.

Reach your conclusion

Decide what the evidence establishes, what remains uncertain and what additional information you would need to change your assessment.

The standard: conclusions should follow the evidence. Evidence should not be selected merely to defend a predetermined conclusion.
How every question is examined

Fourteen questions

Questions citizens can examine for themselves

01

Did the 2020 election challenges establish a legal basis to change the presidential outcome?

Examine state certifications, recounts and audits where applicable, litigation, procedural rulings, merits rulings, evidence offered in particular proceedings, Electoral College results and the congressional count. A case dismissed for standing or another procedural reason should not be described as a merits adjudication of every allegation.

02

What authority did the Vice President have on January 6?

Compare the Constitution, Twelfth Amendment, Electoral Count Act framework in effect in 2021, the Vice President's January 6 position, competing legal arguments, congressional practice and later statutory reforms. Keep the law applicable in 2021 distinct from changes enacted afterward.

03

What caused the breach of the U.S. Capitol?

Build a synchronized chronology using video, communications, speeches, movement of crowds, perimeter breaches, law-enforcement records and prosecutions. Sequence can establish timing; timing alone does not establish causation.

04

What responsibility did President Donald Trump bear?

Examine public statements, the Ellipse speech, communications, conduct during the attack, impeachment proceedings, congressional findings, criminal allegations and relevant judicial rulings. Keep political judgment, congressional judgment, criminal allegations and adjudicated facts distinct.

05

Did federal and local agencies have sufficient warning?

Compare intelligence actually available before January 6 with what was shared, how it was assessed, who received it, what operational decisions followed and what later oversight reviews concluded.

06

Why was the Capitol not better protected?

Examine Capitol Police planning, staffing, intelligence, equipment, the Capitol Police Board, mutual-aid arrangements, command decisions and the difference between hindsight and information available to decision-makers beforehand.

07

Was the National Guard response unnecessarily delayed?

Separate request time, authorization, mission approval, communication, mobilization, deployment and arrival. Examine the District of Columbia Guard's unique command structure and compare contemporaneous records with later recollections.

08

How should congressional objections to electoral votes be understood?

Distinguish the statutory objection process available to members of Congress from violence or unlawful entry at the Capitol. Examine the objections, debates and recorded votes as part of the constitutional and statutory proceeding.

09

How should competing elector documents be understood?

Compare official state certificates, competing documents, the circumstances of their creation, representations accompanying them, communications among participants and subsequent legal proceedings. Avoid applying one characterization indiscriminately to materially different facts without examining the record.

10

Were January 6 defendants treated fairly?

Examine charges, detention decisions, plea agreements, trials, sentences, appellate rulings, comparable federal sentencing principles, later Supreme Court decisions and clemency. Individual defendants should be evaluated by their own conduct and procedural history.

11

What did later Supreme Court decisions actually change?

Study decisions such as Fischer v. United States and Trump v. United States for their actual holdings. A ruling that changes the interpretation of a statute or establishes an immunity framework should not be described as deciding unrelated factual questions it did not adjudicate.

12

How should the January 20, 2025 clemency actions be understood?

Preserve the original charge, plea, verdict and sentence alongside the later pardon or commutation. Executive clemency changes legal consequences; it is analytically different from an appellate court reversing a conviction.

13

What do the pipe bombs tell us?

Treat the pipe-bomb investigation as a separate evidence file. Follow authenticated video, forensic evidence, official investigative statements and later developments without filling gaps with speculation or assuming a relationship to other January 6 events unless evidence establishes one.

14

What should America learn from January 6?

Consider election administration, lawful challenges, political rhetoric, institutional preparedness, peaceful protest, political violence, constitutional succession, public trust, accountability, historical memory and the responsibilities citizens themselves carry in a republic.

Intellectual discipline

What responsible inquiry requires

Separate allegation from proof.
An accusation is a claim until evidence and the applicable process establish more.
Separate source from interpretation.
The same authentic document can support competing interpretations; show the document first.
Separate procedure from merits.
Why a court ended a case matters when describing what the decision established.
Separate chronology from causation.
One event preceding another does not by itself prove why the later event occurred.
Update when the record changes.
Later evidence, appellate decisions and corrected records must remain visible.
Apply the same standard consistently.
Evidence should not become stronger or weaker merely because it favors a preferred political conclusion.
Examine the underlying record

Do not stop with our summary.

Move backward through the project to examine the Evidence Ledger, oral histories, court records, security findings, chronology and constitutional framework. Where possible, follow the source to the original institution that created it.
