THE ESSENTIAL DISTINCTION

Certification is a state process. Seating is a congressional responsibility.

There is no joint Electoral College-style vote approving congressional winners. Each chamber judges the elections, returns, and qualifications of its own members under Article I, Section 5. The President does not independently certify congressional winners or decide who serves. [1]

STATE AUTHORITY

Certification

Officials formally attest to election results under applicable state law.

HOUSE OR SENATE

Seating

Credentials and constitutional eligibility establish a claim to membership.

DISPUTED ENTITLEMENT

Election contest

Evidence is examined to determine who is entitled to the seat.

TWO-THIRDS VOTE

Expulsion

A chamber removes one of its seated members through a separate process.

01 / FROM VOTE TO VOICE

The path to representation

Exact deadlines, recount rules, and certification responsibilities vary by state. These steps describe the general process, not a single national timetable. [2]

  1. 01

    Vote and report

    Voters cast ballots. Election-night totals are preliminary; a media projection is not official certification.

  2. 02

    Canvass and verify

    Election officials reconcile records, verify totals, and process eligible outstanding ballots under state law.

  3. 03

    Review disputed issues

    Audits, recounts, and court proceedings occur as authorized. Their timing and effect on certification depend on the law.

  4. 04

    Certify official results

    The designated state or local authority completes certification. Issuance of a candidate’s election certificate can be a distinct step.

  5. 05

    Present credentials

    Official documentation is presented to the appropriate congressional officers. Valid credentials support the claim to a seat.

  6. 06

    Consider challenges and take the oath

    The relevant chamber addresses membership disputes. Members take the required oath before exercising legislative duties. A contest may continue after seating.

Why it matters

A delay affects more than a candidate. It can leave a community without its full voting representation in Congress.

02 / EVIDENCE AND FAIR PROCEDURE

How an election can be challenged

Before certification

Authorized officials and courts address recounts, ballot disputes, and other claims under applicable law. A demand letter or filed lawsuit is not itself an order stopping certification.

House of Representatives

Under the Federal Contested Election Act, a candidate claiming the seat generally must file with the Clerk and serve a specific notice of contest within 30 days after the authorized declaration of results. The Committee on House Administration considers evidence; the full House decides the contest. Objections may also arise at swearing-in. [3]

United States Senate

A petition or Senate resolution can raise a challenge. The Senate may investigate while seating a senator-elect “without prejudice,” reserving the final entitlement question. The House’s statutory contest process does not govern Senate elections. [4]

A constitutional boundary

In Powell v. McCormack (1969), the Supreme Court held that the House could not add qualifications to exclude a duly elected, constitutionally qualified member. A dispute over who actually won remains a separate question. [5]

03 / THE HISTORICAL RECORD

When representation was interrupted

These cases show different legal actions. They do not create a general power to reject an unfavorable outcome.

2018–2019 · North Carolina’s 9th DistrictCertification withheld

The State Board did not certify the Harris–McCready contest during an absentee-ballot investigation. A court declined to order certification. After an evidentiary hearing, the bipartisan board unanimously ordered a new election. This was noncertification, not expulsion. [6]

2008–2009 · Minnesota SenateElection certificate delayed

The Franken–Coleman contest delayed issuance of the election certificate. The Minnesota Supreme Court resolved the dispute June 30, 2009; Franken took the oath July 7. Certification of vote totals and issuance of a certificate to the winner should not be confused. [7]

1984–1985 · Indiana’s 8th DistrictCertified winner not seated

Indiana declared McIntyre the winner by 34 votes. The House initially seated neither candidate. After its own recount found McCloskey ahead by four votes, the House seated him May 1, 1985. The decision was sharply disputed along party lines. [8]

1974–1975 · New Hampshire SenateUnresolved contest; new election

The Durkin–Wyman dispute left New Hampshire with one serving senator for more than seven months. On July 30, 1975, the Senate voted to declare the seat vacant effective August 8. A temporary appointment and a September special election followed. This was not an expulsion. [9]

1967–1969 · Adam Clayton Powell Jr.Unconstitutional exclusion

The House excluded Powell following his reelection. In 1969 the Supreme Court held the exclusion unconstitutional. The Court did not treat the action as an expulsion merely because the vote exceeded two-thirds. [5]

04 / A SEPARATE CONSTITUTIONAL POWER

How expulsion works

Article I, Section 5, Clause 2 permits each chamber to expel its own members. Expulsion removes a seated member and requires two-thirds of those present and voting, with a quorum present. The President has no approval or veto role. [10]

  1. A resolution proposes expulsion, or an investigation produces a recommendation.
  2. The chamber considers evidence. An ethics investigation is common, but a completed committee investigation is not an express constitutional prerequisite.
  3. The chamber debates and votes under its procedures.
  4. If the two-thirds threshold is met, the member is removed and the seat becomes vacant.

A criminal conviction is not constitutionally required and does not automatically expel a member. Censure and resignation are different from expulsion.

Understand the vote

Illustration assumes a quorum is present. “Present” votes and absences are not yes-or-no votes. This calculator does not determine whether a quorum exists.

Actual expulsion: George Santos

On December 1, 2023, the House expelled Santos by 311–114, with two voting present. He had not yet been criminally convicted. By contrast, Harrison Williams and Bob Packwood resigned before Senate expulsion votes. [11] [12]

05 / REPRESENTATIVE STEWARDSHIP

What citizens can do

Verify the status

Ask whether a report concerns preliminary totals, certified results, a complaint, a court order, or a congressional decision.

Follow the evidence

Identify the decision-maker, legal authority, specific evidence, requested remedy, and effect on representation.

Participate lawfully

Use official election resources, public meetings, authorized observation, and complaint procedures. Reject threats and harassment.

The people’s choice deserves accurate counting, lawful certification, fair review, and constitutional representation.

The Charter’s civic commitment: accuracy, evidence, transparency, equal treatment, timely decisions, and continuity of representation. These are advocated civic standards, not a claim that every item is a uniform legal requirement.
