Part III · Memory, Record & Republic

JANUARY 6, 2021

A Day in the Life of the Republic

January 6 unfolded simultaneously across the Ellipse, the Capitol, congressional chambers, the Vice President's security detail, law-enforcement command structures and public communications. This chronology places those events beside one another so that sequence is not mistaken for causation and individual facts are not separated from their context.

Synchronized chronology

Congress, the rally, the Capitol and the constitutional count

How to read this page

Times are drawn from official congressional records and government investigative materials. Where official sources use approximate times or differ slightly, the page uses “approximately” rather than presenting false precision. Labels identify the institutional track involved.

Morning — January 6

Washington prepares for the electoral count and demonstrations

PUBLIC DEMONSTRATIONSSECURITY

Supporters of President Donald Trump gathered in Washington while Congress prepared for the constitutionally required electoral-vote count. Security and law-enforcement agencies were positioned around the Capitol and elsewhere in the city.

Late morning

The Ellipse rally begins

PRESIDENTRALLY

President Trump addressed supporters near the White House. His remarks repeated claims concerning the election, criticized Vice President Mike Pence and called on supporters to march toward the Capitol. The legal and political significance of the speech became the subject of later impeachment proceedings, investigations and continuing debate.

Approximately 12:53 p.m.

West Front security perimeter is breached

CAPITOLLAW ENFORCEMENTCROWD

A crowd breached outer security barriers on the West Front of the Capitol and confronted police. Official investigative records place this initial perimeter breach before President Trump's Ellipse speech had concluded.

1:00 p.m.

Congress convenes to count electoral votes

CONGRESSCONSTITUTIONAL PROCESS

The House and Senate met in joint session with Vice President Pence presiding to open and count the electoral certificates submitted by the states.

Approximately 1:10 p.m.

President Trump's Ellipse speech concludes

PRESIDENTRALLY

The President concluded his speech. By this time, the initial West Front perimeter breach had already occurred. Additional rally attendees subsequently moved toward the Capitol, joining people already present there.

Important chronological distinction

12:53 p.m. and 1:10 p.m.

Official government records place the initial West Front barrier breach at approximately 12:53 p.m. and the conclusion of President Trump's speech at approximately 1:10 p.m. This sequence is important evidence. By itself, however, chronology does not resolve every question concerning planning, intent, influence, legal responsibility or the conduct of different individuals. Those questions require examination of the broader record.

Early afternoon

Arizona electoral votes are challenged

CONGRESSELECTORAL COUNT

Representative Paul Gosar objected to Arizona's electoral votes, and Senator Ted Cruz joined the written objection. Under the law then governing the count, the House and Senate withdrew to consider the objection separately.

Approximately 1:49 p.m.

Capitol Police request National Guard assistance

SECURITYNATIONAL GUARD

As conditions deteriorated, U.S. Capitol Police leadership sought immediate National Guard assistance. The request, approval chain, mission authorization and deployment became major subjects of later investigations.

Shortly after 2:00 p.m.

The Capitol building is breached

CAPITOLLAW ENFORCEMENT

Individuals forced entry into the Capitol as officers attempted to defend entrances and interior areas. The congressional electoral-count proceeding could no longer continue normally.

Approximately 2:14 p.m.

Security crisis reaches the Vice President and Congress

VICE PRESIDENTCONGRESSSECRET SERVICE

The Vice President's protective detail reported that the building had been breached. Members, staff and the Vice President were protected, evacuated or moved to secure locations as the situation developed.

2:24–2:25 p.m.

Presidential message and Pence relocation

PRESIDENTVICE PRESIDENT

At 2:24 p.m., President Trump posted a message criticizing Vice President Pence. At approximately 2:25 p.m., the Vice President and his protective detail left his Senate office for a secure location inside the Capitol complex.

Afternoon

Law enforcement fights to regain control

LAW ENFORCEMENTFEDERAL RESPONSE

Capitol Police, Metropolitan Police and responding federal and National Guard personnel worked through the afternoon to secure the Capitol and surrounding grounds. The response involved multiple agencies and command structures and is examined separately in Part IV.

4:17 p.m.

President Trump releases a video message

PRESIDENTPUBLIC COMMUNICATION

The President released a recorded video telling people at the Capitol to go home while also repeating claims concerning the election. The full communication should be considered in context rather than reduced to an isolated phrase.

Evening

The Capitol is secured and Congress prepares to return

CAPITOLCONGRESS

After law enforcement regained control, congressional leaders prepared to resume the electoral count rather than abandon the constitutional proceeding.

Evening — January 6

The Senate and House return

CONGRESSCONTINUITY OF GOVERNMENT

Congress reconvened. The Senate rejected the Arizona objection 93–6; the House rejected it 303–121. The joint session then continued the electoral count. A later objection to Pennsylvania also failed.

Early morning — January 7

The electoral count is completed

CONSTITUTIONAL PROCESSOFFICIAL RECORD

Congress completed the count and formally recorded the electoral result: 306 votes for Joseph R. Biden Jr. and 232 for Donald J. Trump. The proceeding that had been interrupted on January 6 was ultimately resumed and completed.

The constitutional fact

The process was interrupted. It was not abandoned.

The attack forced Congress to stop its work for hours, but the House and Senate returned that same evening and continued the count. The continuity of the constitutional process is central to understanding January 6 as both a security crisis and a test of republican institutions.
