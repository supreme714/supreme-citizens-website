COMMUNITY OF CONSENSUS · FIRST PILOT CONVERSATION

Citizenship & Election Participation

What responsibilities do we share as citizens for maintaining meaningful participation in American elections and representative self-government?

This demonstration shows how a future moderated Community of Consensus forum can move from shared information to preliminary positions, deliberation, common ground, final assessment, dissent, and civic action.

DEMONSTRATION MODE
This page does not collect or publish votes. Interactive public voting should not begin until authentication, privacy, duplicate-participation controls, moderation, data-retention rules, accessibility review, and methodology standards are in place.

Step 1 · Learn

Start with shared civic foundations

Participation

Eligible citizens participate through voting and may also engage before and after Election Day through civic education, public meetings, petitions, communication with representatives, community service, and other lawful activity.

Lawful Procedures

Election administration is governed by federal and state constitutional and statutory requirements. Disputes should be addressed through established legal and constitutional processes.

Reliable Information

Participants should distinguish verified information from interpretation, opinion, prediction, and unsupported claims before reaching conclusions.

Rights & Dissent

Citizens may disagree strongly about election policy. The process protects lawful dissent and does not require ideological conformity.

Public record model

Consensus and dissent belong side-by-side

What We Agreed Upon

When a proposition reaches the predetermined threshold, the forum can publish its exact language, support level, sources considered, and recommended civic action.

Where We Still Disagree

Significant objections should remain visible through a Dissenting Statement or fair summary. A minority position is not erased simply because consensus exists.

Step 6 · Act

Turn agreement into participation

Before Election Day

Learn about offices and issues, verify registration and procedures, attend forums, compare candidates, and make a voting plan.

On or Before Election Day

Participate according to applicable law, help eligible family and community members find official voting information, and respect lawful procedures.

After Election Day

Follow certification and public institutions, communicate with representatives, monitor policy, attend meetings, and remain civically engaged.

Evaluate

Review what happened, whether officials responded, whether circumstances changed, and whether the community should reconvene.
