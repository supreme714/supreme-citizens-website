Citizens Branch · Community of Consensus

From Civic Identity to Civic Action

The Supreme Citizens Charter proposes a continuous civic pathway: learn about constitutional government, participate in elections, deliberate with fellow citizens, find genuine common ground, act through lawful civic channels, evaluate government response, and participate again.

Learn → Participate → Deliberate → Find Common Ground → Act → Hold Accountable → Participate Again

Election participation pathway

Before Election Day → Election Day → After Election Day

Before Election Day

Learn about offices and issues. Evaluate candidates. Verify important claims. Know registration and voting rules. Attend civic discussions and candidate forums.

Make a voting plan →

On or Before Election Day

Participate according to applicable law. Vote when eligible. Help family and community members locate reliable official voting information. Respect lawful procedures and fellow citizens.

Election Day Initiative →

After Election Day

Follow certification and lawful challenges. Monitor those elected to office. Communicate priorities. Participate in public processes. Prepare for the next cycle of citizenship.

Follow the ballot-to-oath process →

Community of Consensus

Put the pathway into practice

The first demonstration conversation focuses on Citizenship & Election Participation. Rather than asking participants to accept one broad political position, it separates the subject into individual propositions so citizens can discover where agreement exists and where legitimate disagreement remains.

Explore the Election Participation Pilot Start with the Consensus Framework

Methodology notice: Community of Consensus results describe the participating community. They are not scientific measures of public opinion unless a separately identified representative methodology is used. The current pilot is demonstration-only and does not collect votes.
