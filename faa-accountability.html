Funding, schedule and audit controls

GAO reported that in June 2026 the FAA's high-level Phase 1 systems estimate was about $10.615 billion, compared with about $9.030 billion allocated for those systems, a gap of about $1.585 billion. GAO reported approximately $10.2 billion for Phase 2 systems excluding facilities and no Phase 2 completion date. These estimates are not an additive, comprehensive lifecycle cost.

GAO found that FAA lacked a comprehensive, documented lifecycle cost estimate and an integrated master schedule. Phase 1 was targeted for December 2028; that target was not a verified completion outcome. FAA reported replacing 2,560 of 5,170 aging copper connections by May 2026.

Congress's FY2026 §119J directed monthly modernization spending plans and briefings. The public-facing register will not treat congressional receipt or acceptance of these plans as verified without records.

Sources: GAO audit and FY2026 appropriations text.

November 13, 2026 nationwide ground stop

Preliminary, investigation open: DOT said telecommunications disruptions impaired communication and radar stability. On November 14, the Secretary described maintenance involving an L3Harris contractor's copper-to-fiber migration and possible router or installation issues, explicitly stating the fault had not been determined. This page does not claim a final technical cause or assign legal responsibility.

Questions for follow-up: change-control and pre-deployment testing; authorization and contractor obligations; communications failover; outage duration and flight impact; corrective actions; and any linkage to funded BNATCS work. Incident investigation records must remain distinct from spending records.

Source: DOT November 14, 2026 statement.

Historical comparison and verification method

The 2021 Bipartisan Infrastructure Law provided $25 billion over five years for aviation infrastructure, including $5 billion for FAA facilities and equipment and $20 billion for airport projects. These component figures must not be added to the $25 billion total. The 2025 modernization funding has a different program scope and should not be presented as a like-for-like annual spending comparison.

For each later quarterly revision, preserve the original proposal, law, dated source, project ID, funding category (requested / enacted / obligated / outlay), cost-estimate scope, milestone, incident evidence, verification date and editorial change log. The existing Transportation Spending Register maintains the broader agency comparison and quarterly collection methodology.

Sources: FAA Bipartisan Infrastructure Law program; GAO audit.
