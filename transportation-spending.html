Seven accountability tracks

1. Presidential budget requests

Record proposed budget authority by fiscal year, agency and account. Preserve the original submission, amendments, and request date. Never label a request “approved.”

Primary: USDOT budget estimates; OMB budget tables.

2. Congressional appropriations

Identify public law, enactment date, annual discretionary appropriation, mandatory contract authority, advance appropriations, rescissions and transfers separately. Attach CRS cross-checks.

Primary: enacted laws; CRS transportation funding.

3. Agency obligations and expenditures

Quarterly balances, new obligations, deobligations, gross and net outlays, prior-year balances, and reporting coverage. No fabricated historical totals: records remain pending until matched to source tables.

Primary: USAspending agency account data; DOT agency financial reports.

4. Program reductions

Track proposed cut, enacted rescission, award cancellation, deobligation and expired temporary authority as different events. Record whether a legal authority permits repurposing.

Sources: appropriations laws, OMB, DOT notices, GAO and program ledgers.

5. Air traffic modernization

Track enacted funding, FAA facilities-and-equipment requests, phase-by-phase cost estimates, implementation milestones, contracting and GAO concerns. Do not sum overlapping accounts.

Sources: FAA BNATCS fact sheet; GAO-26-107992.

6. Completion and safety outcomes

Separate awarded, started, substantially completed and accepted projects. Track miles or assets delivered, controller/technology milestones, delay rates and comparable safety rates, with baselines.

Sources: DOT performance reports; NHTSA preliminary fatalities.

7. Quarterly verification

Each record receives source URL, document title, as-of date, fiscal period, retrieval date, analyst review, revision history and confidence state. Changed or missing evidence is flagged rather than silently overwritten.

Cadence: after each federal fiscal quarter (Dec 31, Mar 31, Jun 30, Sep 30), with a release once sources are available.

Public results and safety

Outcome data must carry a measurement period and indicate whether preliminary or final. Example: NHTSA’s November 10, 2026 early estimate reported 16,750 traffic fatalities in the first half of 2026, compared with 17,175 in the first half of 2025 (a preliminary 2.5% decline). This national outcome is not attributed to one appropriation or president. NHTSA source.

Project-level records will show contract/award ID, location, scope, planned versus actual completion dates, actual funding tied to the project, independently documented deliverables and any material audit findings. No completion claim without primary evidence.

Definitions and data methodology

  1. Scope: Department of Transportation operating administrations; do not silently combine with national transportation spending by states and localities.
  2. Budget request: Administration proposal, not an appropriation. Preserve original budget-year document and account definition.
  3. Budget authority: Legal authority to incur obligations. Distinguish annual appropriations, contract authority, advance, supplemental and transferred authority.
  4. Obligation: A binding commitment, including grants and contracts. Negative deobligations may reverse previous commitments.
  5. Outlay: Cash payment liquidating an obligation. Payments in a given fiscal year may relate to authority enacted in an earlier year.
  6. Comparison rule: Compare like-for-like source scopes in nominal dollars first; label constant-dollar inflation-adjusted views separately. Never interpret enacted minus outlays as unused cash.
  7. Presidential attribution: Show both fiscal year and actual office-holder dates; congressional enactment, agency implementation and outcomes have different institutional responsibilities.
  8. Quality: Every numerical record must include source-document URL, publication date, period, units, accounting basis and status (requested, enacted, obligated, outlay, forecast or estimate).

Read USAspending’s spending definitions and OMB Public Budget Database guide for reconciliation limitations.

Quarterly verification and publication

  2. Validate fiscal period, agency, account, metric, source URL and units; check revisions against previous published snapshots.
  3. Reconcile changed enacted totals with legal authority and CRS notes; keep all unverified fields marked pending.
  4. Require a documented editorial review before making new public claims. Publish a date-stamped change log and retained historical snapshots.
