Phase 4 · Security, Intelligence & Response

SECURITY & RESPONSE RECORD

From warning to response — with each institutional stage kept distinct.

This record connects security and intelligence events to stable J6-E evidence identifiers. A warning is not an operational decision; a request is not an authorization; authorization is not mobilization; and later oversight is not the same as contemporaneous knowledge.

Method

Each J6-S record identifies the institutional stage, the supporting or contextual Evidence Ledger records, and a limitation designed to prevent one stage of the response from being mistaken for another.

J6-S-0001
Pre-event warning environment

Federal agencies identified potential threats of violence

WarningGovernment Finding

Later federal oversight found that multiple agencies identified potential threats before January 6.

Evidence: J6-E-0005 · J6-E-0006 · J6-E-0008

Limitation: General threat information does not establish that an agency possessed precise, actionable knowledge of exactly how the attack would unfold.

J6-S-0002
Intelligence processing & sharing

Threat information was not uniformly processed or distributed

IntelligenceInformation Sharing

Oversight reviews identified shortcomings in how some threat information was evaluated, disseminated, or shared with operational partners.

Evidence: J6-E-0005 · J6-E-0006

Limitation: A later finding about information-sharing weakness does not mean every relevant agency or official had the same information at the same time.

J6-S-0003
Security preparation

Capitol planning did not fully reflect the potential for an extreme attack

USCPPreparation

Congressional and government reviews identified weaknesses involving planning, intelligence dissemination, staffing, equipment, and contingency preparation.

Evidence: J6-E-0005 · J6-E-0008

Limitation: Institutional preparedness findings should not be converted into claims about the intent or conduct of individual officers.

J6-S-0004
Emergency assistance request

Requests for outside assistance became urgent as conditions deteriorated

RequestEmergency Response

As the Capitol perimeter and building came under increasing pressure, Capitol Police leadership and partner agencies moved toward emergency mutual aid and National Guard assistance.

Evidence: J6-E-0007 · J6-E-0008

Limitation: The time a request was made must not be represented as the time assistance was authorized, mobilized, or physically deployed.

J6-S-0005
Authorization & command

The D.C. National Guard operated through a federal command structure

AuthorizationNational Guard

The District of Columbia National Guard's command structure required federal action between a request for assistance and execution of a Capitol mission.

Evidence: J6-E-0008

Limitation: Authorization is a distinct stage and should not be collapsed into either the initial request or the later arrival of personnel.

J6-S-0006
Mobilization & mission preparation

Personnel had to be notified, equipped, briefed, and moved

MobilizationMission

Once authority and mission requirements were established, Guard personnel still required time to assemble, receive equipment and instructions, and move toward the Capitol.

Evidence: J6-E-0008

Limitation: Mobilization time is not by itself evidence of why earlier planning or authorization decisions were made.

J6-S-0007
Multi-agency operational response

MPD and other partners reinforced the Capitol response

MPDMutual AidResponse

Metropolitan Police and other outside agencies provided substantial assistance as the emergency expanded beyond Capitol Police capacity.

Evidence: J6-E-0007 · J6-E-0008

Limitation: The presence of responding agencies does not mean their authorities, arrival times, assignments, or command relationships were identical.

J6-S-0008
Later oversight

Reviews identified recurring intelligence, planning, command, and response weaknesses

Later FindingOversight

GAO, congressional, Senate, and other official reviews later documented areas of institutional failure and recommended reforms.

Evidence: J6-E-0005 · J6-E-0006 · J6-E-0007 · J6-E-0008

Limitation: Later oversight benefits from evidence assembled after the event and must not be presented as identical to what decision-makers knew in real time.
