# Supreme Citizens Website — Phase 6 Public Launch & Growth

Launch planning date: October 20, 2026

## Objective

Move SupremeCitizens.org from a standardized production baseline into an intentional public-launch and growth program while preserving its nonpartisan, civic-educational, constitutional framing.

## Launch sequence

### 1. Search discovery

- Confirm `https://supremecitizens.org/sitemap.xml` is publicly reachable after deployment.
- Add and verify `supremecitizens.org` in Google Search Console.
- Submit the production sitemap in Search Console.
- Add the site to Bing Webmaster Tools and submit the same sitemap.
- Monitor indexing rather than repeatedly changing page content in response to normal crawl delays.
- Use canonical `https://supremecitizens.org/` URLs consistently in public outreach.

### 2. Public-facing message

Use this short description consistently when introducing the initiative:

> The Supreme Citizens Charter is a nonpartisan civic and educational framework for constitutional citizenship, equal citizenship, responsible self-government, accountable public stewardship, and the continuing role of We the People in a constitutional republic.

When additional clarification is appropriate:

> “Supreme Citizen” is a civic and educational identity, not a legal status or immunity. The Citizens Branch is an educational concept describing citizens’ continuing civic role; it is not a fourth branch of government. The initiative is not affiliated with sovereign-citizen ideology.

## 3. Primary launch destinations

Public launch communications should direct visitors according to intent:

- First-time visitors → `https://supremecitizens.org/`
- Foundational framework → `https://supremecitizens.org/charter.html`
- Constitutional standards → `https://supremecitizens.org/principles.html`
- Supreme Citizen identity → `https://supremecitizens.org/identity.html`
- Citizens Branch → `https://supremecitizens.org/citizens-branch.html`
- Civic participation → `https://supremecitizens.org/participate.html`
- Media / general inquiries → `https://supremecitizens.org/contact.html`

## 4. Media readiness

Before broad media outreach:

- Confirm `media@supremecitizensbranch.org` is receiving messages.
- Confirm `info@thesupremecitizenscharter.org` is receiving messages.
- Submit one test message through the website contact form and confirm Netlify processing.
- Keep the current press overview synchronized with the official 12 Principles.
- Use the About and Supreme Citizen pages as authoritative explanations of terminology and legal/civic status.
- Direct journalists to the website rather than attaching multiple competing versions of project descriptions.

## 5. Social launch

Recommended launch-message structure:

1. Introduce the Supreme Citizens Charter.
2. Explain that constitutional citizenship involves rights and responsibilities.
3. State that citizens are participants in constitutional self-government, not merely an audience.
4. Invite visitors to read the Charter and 12 Principles.
5. Include one canonical website link.
6. Keep messaging nonpartisan and avoid candidate or party endorsements.

Social channels should use the same logo, project name, website URL, and core description wherever platform fields permit.

## 6. Measurement and privacy

Do not add third-party analytics solely for launch-day vanity metrics.

If analytics are introduced later:

- define the questions the data should answer first;
- use a privacy-conscious implementation;
- document what is collected;
- add or expand privacy/information-use disclosures before collecting additional visitor data;
- avoid collecting sensitive personal information unnecessarily.

Useful non-sensitive launch indicators include:

- search impressions and indexed pages;
- visits to Charter, Principles, Citizens Branch, and Participate pages;
- media inquiries;
- general-information inquiries;
- referral traffic from public outreach;
- legitimate mentions or citations of the initiative.

## 7. First 30 days

### Week 1 — Discovery and controlled launch
- Submit sitemap to search engines.
- Test contact routes.
- Publish introductory social posts.
- Share the homepage with a small initial group and collect usability feedback.

### Week 2 — Civic-resource promotion
- Highlight the Charter and 12 Principles.
- Publish explanatory material about Supreme Citizen terminology.
- Highlight the distinction from sovereign-citizen ideology when relevant.

### Week 3 — Participation
- Promote Citizens Branch and the Citizen Action Guide.
- Highlight Connect With Congress, Voting, and Founding Documents as official-source civic resources.

### Week 4 — Review
- Review search indexing, contact submissions, broken-link reports, mobile feedback, and government-feed reliability.
- Correct verified problems through the normal branch → preview → pull request → merge workflow.
- Avoid redesigning stable pages solely because traffic is initially low.

## 8. Growth principles

Growth should remain consistent with the Charter’s purpose:

- educate before persuading;
- use primary and official sources where practical;
- distinguish civic principles from partisan positions;
- protect equal citizenship and peaceful participation;
- correct factual errors transparently;
- keep constitutional and legal-status disclaimers visible;
- prioritize durable civic resources over short-lived political content.

## 9. Technical maintenance

Continue monitoring:

- White House feed;
- Congress feed/API;
- Supreme Court feed/parser;
- mobile navigation;
- Netlify contact form;
- official external government links;
- sitemap/indexing;
- large image performance.

Future technical enhancements may include automated link checking, accessibility/HTML CI checks, structured data, and conversion of large legacy PNG assets to modern compressed formats.
