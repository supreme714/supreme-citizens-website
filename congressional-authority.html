Congressional Authority & Constitutional Accountability

Three coequal branches. Distinct constitutional powers. A continuing responsibility to the people. Explore what Congress can do, what it cannot do, and how citizens can evaluate the exercise of federal authority.

Foundational question

If Congress makes the laws, how can the branches be equal?

Coequal means independent constitutional standing—not identical powers. Article I vests federal legislative powers in Congress. Article II vests executive power in the President. Article III vests judicial power in the federal courts. Their authority differs, and each is bound by constitutional limits.

“In republican government, the legislative authority necessarily predominates.”
— James Madison, Federalist No. 51 (1788)

Madison recognized the legislature's significant influence and argued for checks against the concentration of power. Congressional authority does not make Congress superior in every constitutional dispute; executive and judicial independence remain essential.

Article I · Congress

Makes federal law, controls appropriations, conducts legislative oversight, and exercises specified checks on other federal officials.

Article II · President

Executes federal law and exercises independent constitutional executive responsibilities, including the veto and other enumerated powers.

Article III · Judiciary

Resolves cases and controversies within federal jurisdiction and exercises independent judicial judgment, including constitutional review.

The Supreme Citizens “Citizens Branch” is a civic participation concept, not a legally established fourth federal branch.

Institutional responsibility

What Congress is empowered to do

Legislate

Enact, amend, and repeal federal statutes through bicameralism and presentment.

Appropriate

Set legally binding federal funding conditions; oversee administration of appropriations within the law.

Investigate

Conduct inquiries tied to legitimate legislative purposes, subject to constitutional rights and privileges.

Check other branches

Exercise impeachment, Senate confirmations, veto overrides, and other specifically assigned constitutional functions.

Important limit: Congressional oversight is not general command authority over executive operations or judicial decisions. Congress also cannot overturn executive action using an unconstitutional one-house legislative veto.

Policy proposal · not enacted law

Congressional Authority and Constitutional Accountability Act of 2027

Proposal status: Supreme Citizens discussion draft only. This proposal has not been introduced in Congress, enacted, or reviewed by congressional legislative counsel. The title is a proposed title, not an official bill number.

The proposed Act seeks clearer statutory delegations, better agency responses to lawful oversight, improved appropriations compliance reporting, and public access to reliable institutional records—while expressly protecting separation of powers.

Title I · Legislative authority

Major delegation accountability statements; structured review of agency statutory authority.

Title II · Oversight

Agency response procedures, record preservation, and lawful dispute-resolution mechanisms.

Title III · Appropriations

Public reporting and corrective-action procedures for material budgetary compliance issues.

Title IV · Public accountability

Source-linked access to laws, votes, public reports, and documented government actions.

Implementation and constitutional safeguards are addressed in the proposed Title V. Some provisions would need further legal drafting, fiscal analysis, and committee review before consideration by Congress.

Research initiative · proposed

Congressional Authority & Accountability Review

This proposed research initiative would maintain a chronological, source-verified record of how Congress uses its constitutional authority. It would complement—but remain distinct from—the Campaign Commitment Register, which tracks campaign statements and subsequent government actions.

1. Identify authority

Cite the constitutional provision, law, or established implied legislative power.

2. Record the action

Capture the date, congressional body, agency or court involved, relevant official documents, and what actually occurred.

3. Verify the outcome

Separate allegations, legal claims, pending decisions, final findings, and unresolved questions.
