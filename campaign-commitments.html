How a record qualifies for publication

1. Original campaign evidence

Identify the candidate, office, election cycle, precise commitment, original date, and an accessible source such as a campaign platform or archived public statement.

2. Government action evidence

Where relevant, link official records such as a bill, vote, executive action, or rulemaking docket. Record the action without attributing unrelated results.

3. Transparent status

Distinguish a documented promise from a related proposal, completed governmental action, or verified outcome. Missing records are not proof of inaction.

4. Corrections and source review

Include review dates and provide a way to challenge inaccurate attribution or outdated records. Corrections should be documented without partisan judgment.
