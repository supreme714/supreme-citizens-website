Article I

The 12 Principles

The Charter's foundational civic principles define the relationship among the people, constitutional institutions, public officials, liberty, accountability, and future generations.

A constitutional civic framework

From Principle to Public Responsibility

The 12 Principles are more than statements of belief. Together they describe how constitutional authority should be understood, how citizens can exercise responsible self-government, and how public institutions and officeholders should exercise delegated power.

Principle→Meaning→Constitutional Connection→Citizen Responsibility→Government Responsibility
01

Popular Sovereignty

Legitimate political authority ultimately originates with the people.

Constitutional Connection

“We the People” establishes the people as the source of the Constitution's authority, while the Constitution structures how that authority is exercised.

What It Requires of Citizens

Learn, vote when eligible, participate peacefully, communicate with government, and accept responsibility for sustaining representative institutions.

What It Requires of Government

Exercise only constitutionally delegated authority and remain accountable to the people through lawful institutions, elections, and constitutional limits.

02

Equal Citizenship

Every citizen possesses equal civic dignity. Political disagreement does not diminish citizenship.

Constitutional Connection

Constitutional government protects equal citizenship through representative government, due process, equal protection, and guarantees of individual rights.

What It Requires of Citizens

Respect the lawful rights and civic standing of others, including people with whom you strongly disagree.

What It Requires of Government

Administer law without treating political loyalty, viewpoint, race, religion, or other protected status as a basis for unequal civic worth.

03

Constitutional Supremacy

No public official, institution, party, movement, corporation, or citizen stands above the Constitution.

Constitutional Connection

The Constitution is the nation's supreme legal framework, and governmental authority is bounded by its structure, rights, amendments, and lawful interpretation.

What It Requires of Citizens

Place constitutional processes and equal rights above personal loyalty to leaders, movements, or parties.

What It Requires of Government

Public officials must act within constitutional authority and remain subject to lawful review, limits, and accountability.

04

Distributed Constitutional Power

Legislative, executive, and judicial powers are divided so that no branch becomes the government itself.

Constitutional Connection

Articles I, II, and III establish separate legislative, executive, and judicial institutions with distinct constitutional functions.

What It Requires of Citizens

Understand which institution holds which powers and resist demands that one office or branch absorb the constitutional role of another.

What It Requires of Government

Each branch should exercise its own authority while respecting the lawful constitutional powers and independence of the others.

05

Unitary but Limited Executive Authority

The President leads the executive branch, while Congress, the judiciary, the states, elections, and constitutional rights remain independent limits.

Constitutional Connection

Article II vests executive power in the President while the broader constitutional structure assigns independent powers to Congress and the judiciary and preserves federalism and individual rights.

What It Requires of Citizens

Distinguish effective executive leadership from unlimited personal authority and evaluate executive action by constitutional standards regardless of party.

What It Requires of Government

The executive should faithfully execute the law while respecting congressional authority, judicial review, constitutional rights, elections, and lawful state authority.

06

Temporary Public Stewardship

Public office is a temporary public trust—not personal ownership, dominion, or sovereignty.

Constitutional Connection

Terms of office, elections, oaths, succession, impeachment, and other constitutional mechanisms reinforce that governmental offices continue beyond their temporary occupants.

What It Requires of Citizens

Treat officeholders as temporary stewards rather than permanent rulers and judge them by their conduct, responsibilities, and constitutional obligations.

What It Requires of Government

Officeholders should use public authority for public purposes, preserve institutional integrity, and support lawful transitions of power.

07

Authority Requires Accountability

The greater the authority entrusted to an officeholder or institution, the greater the obligation to accept responsibility for its use.

Constitutional Connection

Elections, congressional oversight, judicial review, impeachment, transparency laws, and other lawful mechanisms help subject public power to accountability.

What It Requires of Citizens

Ask informed questions, examine outcomes, apply consistent standards, and use lawful civic mechanisms to demand accountability.

What It Requires of Government

Officials and institutions should explain and defend the exercise of public power and accept lawful oversight and consequences.

08

Protected Liberty and Rule of Law

Citizens and officials alike remain subject to lawful constitutional authority, and rights apply even when their exercise is unpopular.

Constitutional Connection

The Bill of Rights and later amendments protect fundamental liberties while due process and equal protection constrain arbitrary governmental action.

What It Requires of Citizens

Exercise rights responsibly and defend constitutional protections for others, including unpopular speakers, groups, and viewpoints.

What It Requires of Government

Enforce law through constitutional procedures and respect protected liberty even when doing so is politically inconvenient.

09

Checks, Balances, and Institutional Independence

Independent institutions and constitutional checks are safeguards against concentrated power.

Constitutional Connection

The constitutional structure gives institutions overlapping checks—such as legislation, vetoes, appointments, confirmations, appropriations, oversight, and judicial review—to restrain concentrated authority.

What It Requires of Citizens

Value institutional independence even when a check frustrates a preferred political outcome.

What It Requires of Government

Institutions should exercise lawful checks in good faith and resist coercion that would convert independent constitutional functions into personal or partisan instruments.

10

Electoral and Civic Accountability

Citizenship does not begin and end at the ballot box; self-government requires engagement before, during, and after elections.

Constitutional Connection

Representative institutions, recurring elections, freedoms of speech, press, assembly, and petition provide channels for continuing democratic accountability.

What It Requires of Citizens

Stay informed between elections, communicate with representatives, attend public meetings, petition, organize peacefully, and vote when eligible.

What It Requires of Government

Protect lawful participation, maintain fair electoral processes, respond to public oversight, and preserve channels through which citizens can petition and criticize government.

11

Constitution Before Party

Political parties organize competition, but they do not possess constitutional sovereignty.

Constitutional Connection

The Constitution establishes offices and institutions rather than granting constitutional sovereignty to political parties.

What It Requires of Citizens

Apply constitutional standards consistently to allies and opponents and refuse to excuse abuses of power solely because they benefit a preferred party.

What It Requires of Government

Officials should discharge constitutional duties to the public and the law rather than treating governmental institutions as property of a political party.

12

Posterity

Each generation holds the Republic in trust and must preserve constitutional institutions for those who follow.

Constitutional Connection

The Constitution's Preamble expressly identifies securing the blessings of liberty to “our Posterity” as part of the constitutional project.

What It Requires of Citizens

Consider long-term institutional consequences, teach civic knowledge, participate responsibly, and avoid sacrificing durable constitutional safeguards for temporary advantage.

What It Requires of Government

Public institutions should preserve constitutional continuity, lawful transitions, institutional capacity, and protected liberty for future generations.

How to use these principles: They are civic and educational standards for thinking about constitutional citizenship and public stewardship. They do not replace the Constitution, create new governmental authority, or provide legal advice. Their purpose is to help citizens ask consistent questions about power, rights, responsibility, accountability, and the preservation of constitutional government.

The constitutional equation

Strong institutions, limited authority, engaged citizens.

Strong Executive + Strong Congress + Independent Judiciary + Protected Rights + Constitutional Limits + Institutional Independence + Engaged Citizens

= Constitutional Government

Not: “Strong Leader = Government.”
