About the initiative

A civic framework for constitutional self-government.

The Supreme Citizens Charter is designed to encourage constitutional literacy, equal civic dignity, independent judgment, peaceful disagreement, institutional accountability, and active participation.

Purpose

The Charter offers a common civic vocabulary that can be used across political, ideological, cultural, and regional differences. Its focus is the constitutional relationship between citizens and government rather than loyalty to a particular party or personality.

It promotes effective government under constitutional accountability—not powerless government, personal sovereignty, or exemption from civic obligations.

Core commitment

We the People • Constitutional Citizenship • Public Stewardship

Public authority is temporary. Citizenship endures. Institutions must remain independent. The Constitution provides the framework. The Republic is ours to protect.

Our civic identity

What Supreme Citizens Means

Supreme Citizens is a civic and educational initiative grounded in constitutional citizenship, equal civic dignity, the rule of law, civic responsibility, and accountable government. The name expresses the principle that in the American constitutional system, legitimate political authority originates with We the People.

Supreme Citizens is not associated with the sovereign citizen movement. The Supreme Citizens Charter does not claim that individuals are above the law, exempt from federal, state, or local law, or possess personal governmental authority. It teaches the opposite: citizens and public officials alike are subject to the Constitution and the rule of law.

The word “Supreme” refers to the collective sovereignty of the people within the constitutional system—not supremacy of one person, group, race, political party, or ideology over another. Every citizen possesses equal civic dignity, and public authority is exercised through constitutional institutions established by the people.

Civic-educational status

A framework for learning and participation

The Charter is a civic and educational document. It does not create legal rights, replace the United States Constitution, supersede federal or state law, substitute for judicial decisions, or provide legal advice. Constitutional references are intended to encourage civic understanding and lawful participation.
