About the initiative

A civic framework for constitutional self-government.

The Supreme Citizens Charter is a nonpartisan civic and educational initiative designed to strengthen constitutional literacy, equal civic dignity, independent judgment, peaceful participation, public stewardship, and accountable government.

Why the Charter exists

Citizenship Does Not End on Election Day

The American constitutional system begins with We the People, but self-government depends on more than periodically choosing officeholders. It requires citizens who understand constitutional institutions, protect liberty, evaluate information, question power, participate peacefully, respect the equal rights of others, and hold public officials accountable under the same constitutional standards.

The Supreme Citizens Charter was created to provide a common civic vocabulary for those responsibilities across political, ideological, cultural, and regional differences.

Mission • Purpose • Vision

A Republic Sustained by Engaged Citizens

Mission

Advance constitutional citizenship by helping people understand their rights, responsibilities, governmental institutions, and continuing role in peaceful democratic self-government.

Purpose

Provide a nonpartisan civic framework connecting popular sovereignty, equal citizenship, constitutional limits, protected liberty, public stewardship, institutional independence, and governmental accountability.

Vision

A constitutional republic in which citizens remain informed and engaged, public institutions remain independent, rights are protected consistently, political disagreement remains peaceful, and public authority is treated as a temporary trust.

Clear by design

What We Are — and What We Are Not

The name “Supreme Citizens” describes the collective role of the people within constitutional government. It does not describe personal supremacy or exemption from law.

What We Are

  • A civic and educational initiative.
  • A framework for constitutional citizenship and public stewardship.
  • Nonpartisan and independent of candidate or party loyalty.
  • Committed to equal civic dignity and the rule of law.
  • Supportive of peaceful speech, voting, petition, assembly, service, oversight, and other lawful participation.
  • Focused on accountable, effective government operating within constitutional limits.

What We Are Not

  • Not a political party or campaign organization.
  • Not a proposed fourth branch of the federal government.
  • Not a claim that individual citizens possess personal governmental sovereignty.
  • Not associated with the sovereign citizen movement.
  • Not a claim that anyone is exempt from courts, taxes, licenses, regulations, or other lawful obligations.
  • Not a replacement for the Constitution, federal or state law, judicial decisions, or professional legal advice.

Our civic identity

What “Supreme Citizens” Means

The term expresses the constitutional principle that legitimate political authority ultimately originates with We the People. The people establish government through the Constitution, while governmental power is exercised through institutions that are created, divided, and limited by that Constitution.

The word “Supreme” refers to collective popular sovereignty within the constitutional system—not supremacy of one individual, race, religion, group, political party, or ideology over another. No Supreme Citizen is supreme over another citizen.

Citizens and public officials alike remain subject to constitutional government and the rule of law. The Charter therefore emphasizes participation rather than exemption, accountability rather than personal sovereignty, and peaceful constitutional processes rather than rejection of lawful institutions.

Core commitments

Standards That Apply Across Political Differences

Constitution Before Party

Constitutional standards should apply consistently whether power is exercised by political allies or political opponents.

Equal Citizenship

Political disagreement does not diminish another person's civic dignity or constitutional protections.

Peaceful Participation

Political change should be pursued through lawful elections, speech, petition, assembly, courts, public advocacy, and other constitutional processes—not political violence.

Public Stewardship

Public office is temporary. Officials exercise entrusted authority; they do not own the institutions they temporarily lead.

Institutional Independence

Checks, balances, federalism, oversight, and independent constitutional institutions protect the Republic from concentrated personal power.

Responsibility to Posterity

Citizens and officials should consider whether today's choices preserve constitutional institutions and liberty for future generations.

Who this initiative is for

A Public Civic-Education Resource

The Supreme Citizens Charter is designed for people who want a clearer, nonpartisan way to study constitutional citizenship and connect civic principles to lawful participation.

Citizens

Use the framework to understand rights, responsibilities, institutions, elections, public stewardship, and practical civic participation.

Educators & Civic Groups

Use the Charter, 12 Principles, founding-document links, and action guide as discussion and civic-learning resources.

Journalists & Researchers

Use the About, Supreme Citizen, media, and source pages to understand the initiative's terminology, status, and constitutional framing.

Public Officials & Community Leaders

Use the same standards of constitutional limits, accountability, equal citizenship, and temporary public stewardship that the framework asks citizens to apply.

Relationship to the Constitution: The Supreme Citizens Charter does not claim authority over the Constitution or law. It is an educational framework for understanding and discussing constitutional citizenship. Legal authority remains with the Constitution, applicable federal and state law, and authoritative judicial interpretation.

Civic-educational status

A Framework for Learning and Participation

The Supreme Citizens Charter is a civic and educational document. It does not create legally enforceable rights, amend or replace the United States Constitution, confer governmental authority, supersede federal or state law, substitute for judicial decisions, or provide legal advice. Constitutional references are intended to encourage civic understanding, informed discussion, and lawful participation.
