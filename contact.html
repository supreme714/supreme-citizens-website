Frequently asked questions

Understanding Supreme Citizens

Concise answers to common questions about the initiative, its terminology, constitutional framework, and participation.

Is the Supreme Citizens Charter a political party?

No. The Supreme Citizens Charter is a nonpartisan civic and educational initiative. It does not endorse candidates or require allegiance to a political party or ideology.

What does “Supreme Citizens” mean?

The term refers to the people collectively as the source of legitimate political authority within the constitutional system. It does not mean that an individual citizen is superior to another person or above the law.

Is the Citizens Branch a fourth branch of government?

No. The Constitution establishes the Legislative, Executive, and Judicial branches of the federal government. “Citizens Branch” is an educational civic concept describing the people's continuing role in learning, voting, petitioning, questioning, serving, evaluating government, and seeking accountability.

Is Supreme Citizens connected to the sovereign citizen movement?

No. The Supreme Citizens Charter is not affiliated with the sovereign citizen movement. The initiative emphasizes participation within constitutional government, equal responsibility under law, lawful institutions, and peaceful civic processes.

Does the Charter replace or amend the U.S. Constitution?

No. The Charter is a civic and educational document. It does not amend, replace, supersede, or reinterpret authoritative constitutional law.

Do participants have to share one political ideology?

No. The framework is intended to apply across political differences. Its emphasis is constitutional citizenship, equal rights, consistent standards, peaceful participation, and accountable public stewardship.

How can someone participate?

Start by learning how constitutional institutions work, knowing your rights and representatives, evaluating information carefully, voting, petitioning, communicating with government, attending public meetings, serving your community, defending equal rights, and using peaceful lawful processes to seek change.

Can the Charter or website provide legal advice?

No. The material is educational and civic in nature and is not a substitute for professional legal advice, applicable law, or authoritative judicial interpretation.
